Cleft Palate 3D and Augmented Reality Model

3D model viewer:

What you're looking at:

Featured above is an interactive 3D model of a skull with a left unilateral cleft palate. For ease of viewing the cleft palate, the lower jaw (the mandible) has been removed from the model. The yellow surfaces show where the bones did not fully fuse together before birth, which has resulted in the defect in the left bones of the upper jaw. The region coloured pink are the left and right maxillae, which are the front of the two bones involved in the cleft. The blue bones are the palatine bones and also feature a complete cleft, which can be tracked from the left maxilla through the left palatine bone.

How to use this 3D model:

Within the 3D model viewer above, click on each orange circle to toggle the name of each bone or region. If using a mobile device or tablet, press with one finger to rotate the model, pinch with two fingers to zoom in or out and press with two fingers and drag to move the position of the model. On desktop web browsers, hold the left-click and drag to rotate the model, scroll to zoom in or out, and hold the shift button with left-click and drag to move the position of the model.

On an iOS or Android mobile device with a camera, tap "View in AR" below to place the life-sized model into the room with you. This will open your camera and allow you to place the coloured skull model in-front of you. You will have the option to scale the model. Once happy with the size and position, you can rotate your camera to view different angles of the model in the space in-front of your device.

Research project:

This model was created for the research project "The Use of 3D Printed and Augmented Reality Models as Educational Aids in Cleft Care: A Comparative Study" under Adelaide University. The aims of this project are to investigate the preferences of patients and clinicians when offered traditional paper-based pamphlets, augmented reality models and 3D printed models. The 3D model was crafted in the application Blender to render the 3D model of a skull with left unilateral cleft palate depicted above. This model will be 3D printed and used as the 3D printed educational tool shared with clinicians and patients.

Model adapted from "Maxillae" by Anatomary (Sketchfab, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), based on BodyParts3D data (Life Sciences Integrated Database Center). Designed for patient education purposes.
